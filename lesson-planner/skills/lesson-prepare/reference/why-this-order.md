# Why this order (short, for the teacher who asks)

- **Topic lead-in before the text**: activating what learners already know makes the text understandable (schema theory).
- **Text before grammar, meaning before form**: learners process meaning first, form second (VanPatten, input
  processing); the structure is met in context before it is named (Schmidt, the noticing hypothesis).
- **The rule is made explicit and confirmed**: explicit instruction beats implicit (Norris & Ortega 2000; Spada &
  Tomita 2010); discovery works only with guidance and feedback (Alfieri et al. 2011).
- **Vocabulary practice after the text**: explicit word work after meeting the word in context helps retention more
  than pre-teaching (Pellicer-Sánchez; Sonbul & Schmitt). Cards come last, as review.
- **A speaking task ends the lesson**: task work builds fluency, drills build accuracy (task-based vs PPP studies;
  Bryfonski & McKay 2019).
- Levels and can-do goals follow the CEFR (Council of Europe, 2001; Companion Volume 2020).
